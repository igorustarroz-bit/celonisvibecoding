celonis / vibecoding Front-end design experiments by Hanzo — unofficial prototypes only

Unofficial prototype. Front-end design experiments by Hanzo exploring improvements to a client website. This is not an official page of the brand shown, it is not published or endorsed by them, and it is not a working product. The experiment pages embed locally saved copies of public pages purely as a visual backdrop: every outbound link in them is disabled, no data is collected and no third-party scripts run. Experiment 4

eBook hero: a 3D book instead of the product shot (WebGL)

The eBook detail page with its static rendered cover replaced by a real 3D book built with the Data Core stack (Three.js r147): long-lens camera placed like the product shot's photographer, near-black cover lit by the same banded softboxes, white light edges, subtle bloom, mouse parallax. Click the book and it opens at the middle on a two-page editorial spread (invented copy, two photos from the site), rising to face you as a wide V; click again to close. Bottom-right: the tuner panel (T hides/shows it). The download and newsletter forms are inert visual replicas. Live knobs in window.BOOK, copy in window.BOOK_SPREAD.

Experiment 5

Celosphere 2026: the seal forms itself on scroll, then settles into the hero (Canvas 2D)

The Celosphere 2026 event page opens on a black stage. As you scroll, the "Celosphere 26" seal assembles in the centre of the viewport: its 57 stripe segments fly in along their own stripes — odd rows from the bottom-left, even rows from the top-right, ordered so nothing ever collides — while the numbers settle out of a Matrix-style flicker. It then slides right, the title rises to meet it, and everything settles into the untouched hero, the seal landing exactly on the original artwork's spot. Every step is a pure function of the scroll, so scrolling up plays it in reverse. Once formed, the seal reacts to the pointer (gyroscope on phones): stripes fatten under it and thin away from it, and near the rim the rows stretch open around the cursor. Geometry extracted from the client's artwork, not redrawn. Bottom-right: the tuner panel (T hides/shows it). The forms are inert visual replicas. Live knobs in window.SEAL.
